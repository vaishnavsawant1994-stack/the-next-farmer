# Field Research & Validation Plan
Version 0.1 | 2026-11-14 | Research not yet conducted

## Purpose
Test whether our proposed solution addresses **real** Indian farmer decisions, not only what a product team imagines. Research must distinguish stated preferences from actual practices, trusted sources, willingness/ability to pay and ability to act.

## Proposed pilot sampling (not statistically representative)
Start with **30–50 farmer interviews** in Maharashtra's Nashik district, including Satana/Baglan, plus 5–10 agricultural advisers and relevant market/service stakeholders. Expand to other districts/crops before national claims.

Quota targets are hypotheses; adjust with community partners:
- At least 10 marginal and 10 small operated holdings; record size in local units, **not land title**.
- Include tenant/sharecropper farmers, at least 10 women directly involved in decisions, young farmers and farmers with older/shared phones.
- Include rainfed and irrigated farms; onion growers, field-crop growers, horticulture and an allied-livelihood subgroup.
- Recruit varied digital literacy, income constraints and local-language preference.
- Interview 3–5 KVK/extension workers, 2–3 input/service providers and 2 market/FPO stakeholders separately.
- Sampling should not be drawn only from smartphone-savvy or well-connected farmers.

## Consent and research ethics
Explain who is conducting research and why, record verbal/written informed consent, allow interview to stop without penalty, and obtain separate consent for photographs/audio. Never request Aadhaar, bank passwords, full land-record extracts or sensitive identifiers. Use anonymized study IDs; store notes securely with access restrictions and deletion schedule. Do not upload identifiable field interviews to a public GitHub repository.

## Interview guide (farmer)
### Background
1. What crops/livestock activities did you undertake in the last year?
2. What land area do you operate (owned/leased/shared), irrigation type and approximate seasonal water availability?
3. Who chooses crops, inputs, irrigation, buyers and borrowing decisions?
4. Which language, phone/device and internet access do you normally use? Is the device shared?

### Before planting
5. How do you decide crop and variety? Whose advice do you trust and why?
6. What went wrong with the last planting decision, if anything?
7. How do you estimate total investment? Show the last calculation if comfortable.
8. Which seed/input purchases are hardest to evaluate?

### During cultivation
9. What did you last ask another farmer, shopkeeper, adviser or KVK about?
10. How did you identify the most recent crop disease, insect or deficiency problem?
11. What evidence did you use before applying a chemical or fertilizer?
12. What causes irrigation uncertainty and what information would help?
13. Describe a weather-related loss; what warning arrived, and when?

### At sale and after
14. How did you choose the last buyer/mandi? What did grading, commission and transport cost?
15. Were you paid when expected? What proof of the transaction did you retain?
16. What is the biggest source of avoidable loss (₹) over one crop cycle?
17. Which schemes or formal services did you attempt to use and what blocked you?

### Advice and product fit
18. Which 3 farmer problems would you want solved first and why?
19. Show how you currently obtain information (phone calls, WhatsApp, videos, meetings, extension officers).
20. Would you trust guidance without a visible source or named adviser? What proof would change that?
21. Try a simple crop-calendar prototype: can you complete one task in your chosen language?
22. Try a cost entry: do rupee, acre/guntha/hectare, kg/quintal units make sense?
23. What should happen if the app does not know the answer?
24. Would you use an SMS, WhatsApp, app notification or voice call? How much data/airtime does it require?
25. Which parts must be free? Is there any service you already pay for and would pay again?

## Field observation tasks
- Observe actual calculation of farm inputs/returns, not just interview descriptions.
- Watch farmer navigate location selection, crop stage, add expense and obtain source details.
- Test without strong mobile signal and on a low-end device.
- Document literacy/numeracy obstacles and whether a helper is needed.
- Compare farmer's chosen remedy with verified advice and measure misconceptions without blaming farmer.

## Expert protocol
- Review one crop guide and one symptom flow with KVK/agronomy expert.
- Verify that any dosing or chemical advice is tied to a jurisdiction-specific approved label and latest approved source.
- Evaluate escalation cases (suspected severe pest, crop failure, chemical exposure, animal illness).
- Measure reviewer workload and turnaround; decide realistic service-level objectives.

## Synthesis method
Maintain an anonymized evidence table:
| ID | Segment | Crop | Problem | Frequency (self report) | Severity ₹/time | Workaround | Unmet need | Source note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
Use only consented anonymized responses. Tag problems by decision stage, source, confidence, water constraints, land relationship and preferred channel.

Prioritize by: severity of potential harm, observed frequency, achievable impact, solution feasibility, trust, inclusion, data availability, and operating cost. Avoid treating interview counts as population-level prevalence.

## Validation experiments and gates
1. **Discovery gate:** 30+ interviews completed with segment coverage; top 5 problems evidence-linked.
2. **Comprehension gate:** 80%+ of a minimum 15 relevant pilot farmers can understand a reviewed crop-stage instruction without facilitator help (proposed target).
3. **Usability gate:** 80%+ complete plot + crop + one expense using a low-end handset with limited assistance; track time and errors (proposed target).
4. **Trust gate:** users can identify source date and escalation route after scenario review.
5. **Safety gate:** agronomist review and red-team checks produce no unblocked unsafe prescriptions in high-risk test cases.
6. **Value gate:** users return for a real crop-cycle task (task completion and retention), not mere sign-up.
7. **Operational gate:** an expert can resolve/redirect a realistic pilot case within agreed SLA.
All numeric thresholds are **proposed acceptance targets**, not measured results.

## Deliverables
- Anonymized needs map and ranked problem backlog.
- Farmer journey artifacts and observed workflow diagrams.
- Marathi/Hindi terminology glossary and accessibility findings.
- Pilot crop/scope decision with expert sign-off.
- Cost-to-serve estimate and service-level limits.
- Revision of FR IDs and roadmap based on evidence.
